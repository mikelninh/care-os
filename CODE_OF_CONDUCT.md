# CareOS Community Code of Conduct

CareOS brings together engineering, clinical practice, design, security, policy and lived experience.

That only works if people can disagree rigorously without making the space hostile.

## Our standard

Be:

- **kind** — assume other contributors are here to help;
- **specific** — critique code, evidence, workflows and assumptions rather than people;
- **curious** — healthcare disciplines see different failure modes;
- **honest** — match claims to evidence and disclose uncertainty;
- **inclusive** — expertise can come from clinicians, engineers, patients, carers, researchers, operators and newcomers;
- **safety-minded** — never trade patient safety, privacy or dignity for speed or technical novelty.

## Healthy disagreement is welcome

Examples:

- “This architecture will fail under partial FHIR pagination; here is a fixture.”
- “This UI makes a pending result look resolved; here is a safer interaction.”
- “I disagree with this agent boundary because the tool proxy still permits X.”
- “This metric overstates the evidence at n=4.”

That is useful.

Personal attacks, harassment, discrimination, intimidation, deliberate humiliation or repeatedly bad-faith behaviour are not.

## Healthcare-specific responsibility

Do not post identifiable patient data, credentials, private hospital documents or sensitive information you do not have permission to share.

Do not pressure clinicians or patients to disclose protected information for the sake of a demo or issue report.

Do not present synthetic or formative evidence as clinical validation.

## When something goes wrong

If a conversation becomes heated, return to:

1. What is the concrete problem?
2. What evidence do we have?
3. What could falsify our assumption?
4. What is the smallest safe experiment or test?

For security-sensitive findings, follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) instead of publishing exploit details in a public issue.

## The spirit

> **We are all in this together.**

Nobody owns all the context required to improve healthcare systems. The project gets stronger when people can bring different expertise, challenge each other respectfully and leave the evidence better than they found it.
